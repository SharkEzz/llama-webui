The monitor samples `llama-server` and the machine on a fixed interval, whether a browser is connected or not. Each
tick produces a `Snapshot`, appends one point to every history series, and is broadcast over the WebSocket. The loop
lives in `app/server/monitor/`, the readers of each data source in `app/server/collectors/`.

| File                                                 | Role                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `app/server/monitor/Monitor.ts`                      | `Monitor`: the service of the ticks, its latest `Snapshot` and the history.                                            |
| `app/server/monitor/tick.ts`                         | `beginTick()` and `endTick()`: what a tick derives from its readings, the series it feeds, the snapshot.               |
| `app/server/monitor/gpus.ts`                         | `collectGpus()`: every card, with `llama-server`'s VRAM on it.                                                         |
| `app/server/monitor/History.ts`                      | `History` (named rings plus timestamps), `Ring` and `historyPoints()`.                                                 |
| `app/server/monitor/Placement.ts`, `placementLog.ts` | `Placement` and `parsePlacement()`: how the model is split across devices, from the logs.                              |
| `app/server/llama/probes.ts`                         | `readLlama()`: `/health`, `/slots` and `/metrics` of a `llama-server`, through `LlamaHttp`.                            |
| `llamacpp-js/metrics`                                | `parseSlots()`, `parsePrometheus()` and the math on `/slots` and `/metrics` ([llamacpp-js](/developers/llamacpp-js/)). |
| `app/server/collectors/Collectors.ts`                | `Collectors`: builds the readers and follows the settings.                                                             |

| `app/server/collectors/amdgpu.ts`                    | AMD cards through the `amdgpu` sysfs files and `/proc/<pid>/fdinfo`.                                                   |

The user-facing pages are the [Dashboard](/docs/dashboard/), [System](/docs/system/) and
[Topology](/docs/topology/); the routes are in [Monitor and system](/developers/api/monitor/).

## The tick loop

`Monitor.layer` builds the monitor with the other services (`app/server/App.ts`). Building it returns at once, so
the API is ready before any snapshot exists: a fiber of the layer's scope builds the collectors, in a scope of their
own, then runs the ticks. A failure to build them is logged and leaves the monitor without snapshots. At shutdown,
the end of the layer closes the collectors' scope, which stops their programs at once (1 s at most for one stuck in a
driver call), before `llama-server` stops ([Architecture](/developers/architecture/#shutdown)).

Each tick comes `pollIntervalMs` (1,000 ms by default, 250 to 60,000 ms) after the start of the previous one, at once
when the previous one took longer: one tick at a time, so a slow source never stacks ticks. A tick that fails is
logged (`monitoring tick`) and the next one still comes; a reader that fails gives its fallback (`null` or an empty
list) and leaves the rest of the tick intact.

Each tick (`Monitor.tick`):

1. Reads `supervisor.state`. A server is active in `starting`, `ready`, `stopping` and `external`; its PID comes from
   the state (it can be `null` for an external server).
2. `beginTick()`: when the PID differs from the previous tick (restart, other profile, stop), resets everything
   derived from the previous process: the last `/slots` and `/metrics` readings, `lastRequest`, `spec`,
   `recentRequests`. Counters are never diffed across two processes.
3. Reads, all at once: `readLlama()` on the active server, every card with the process VRAM of the PID
   (`collectGpus()`), `/proc/<pid>`, the DRAM bandwidth, the machine's CPU and memory, and the disks.
4. `endTick()`: derives rates, request stats and queue figures, pushes one point per series into the history (`0`
   to the `llama-server` series and to `proc.cpuPct` with no active server; a gap to every series not fed), and
   builds the snapshot, every number in it finite.
5. Publishes the snapshot as a `snapshot` message on the WebSocket hub.

`configure` is called after `PATCH /api/settings` saved a change. It reads the stored settings under a lock, so two
changes saved at once end on the last one; the collectors apply them first, and only then do they become current:
the history is resized when `historySeconds` or `pollIntervalMs` changes (the most recent points are kept), and the
wait for the next tick follows the new interval. A change refused by the collectors answers `CollectorsNotApplied`
(the settings stay saved).

## The Snapshot

`Snapshot` (`packages/webui-shared/src/monitor.ts`) is the whole state of one tick, serializable as is.

| Group                  | Fields                                                                             | Source                                  |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Tick                   | `at` (ms), `tick` (counter since start)                                            | Monitor                                 |
| `llama-server`         | `health`, `rates`, `slots`, `kv`, `queue`, `lastRequest`, `spec`, `recentRequests` | `/health`, `/slots`, `/metrics`         |
| GPUs                   | `gpus` (used by the server), `allGpus` (every card, sorted by `busId`)             | NVML or `nvidia-smi`, `amdgpu` sysfs    |
| `llama-server` process | `proc`: `cpuPct`, `rssBytes`, `dramBps`                                            | `/proc/<pid>/stat` and `status`, `perf` |
| Machine                | `system` (`SystemSample`, with `memBw`), `disk` (`DiskSample`)                     | `/proc`, `/sys`, `perf`                 |
| History                | `points`, `history` (`intervalMs`, `capacity`)                                     | `History`                               |

Details worth knowing:

- `health` is `ok` (`/health` answered `200`), `loading` (`503`), `error` (another status), `down` (no answer), or
  `null` when no server is active. `/slots` and `/metrics` are only read when it is `ok`.
- `rates` (`genTps`, `ppTps`) come from `/slots`: for each processing slot, the growth of its decoded and processed
  prompt tokens since the previous tick (the whole count when the slot started a new task), divided by the elapsed
  time.
- `kv` adds up `n_prompt_tokens` and `n_ctx` over the slots. The code notes that the `llama.cpp` build it targets has
  no `kv_cache_*` metrics.
- `queue` reads `llamacpp:requests_processing`, `llamacpp:requests_deferred` and `llamacpp:n_busy_slots_per_decode`.
- `lastRequest` and `recentRequests` (at most 40, newest first) come from the growth of the `/metrics` counters between
  two ticks (`tokens_predicted_total`, `tokens_predicted_seconds_total`, `prompt_tokens_total`, `prompt_seconds_total`,
  `prompt_tokens_cached_total`, the speculative decoding counters). `llama-server` publishes the prompt counters when the prompt is done and the generation counters when the
  request ends, so a delta with a prompt opens a request and a generation-only delta completes the current one
  (`mergeRequestStats()`, `foldRecent()`). With several busy slots, these are per-tick approximations.
- `spec` holds the cumulative speculative decoding counters: drafts, drafted and accepted tokens, the acceptance
  rate, and the acceptance per draft position.
- `gpus` keeps the cards where the `llama-server` process holds more than `USED_GPU_THRESHOLD_BYTES` (64 MiB). It is
  empty when the PID is unknown.
- Hidden cards (the `hiddenGpus` setting) and runtime-suspended AMD cards appear in `allGpus` with `hidden` or
  `suspended` set and no readings: they are never read.

## History

`History` keeps one `Ring` per series key, all with the same capacity, plus a ring of the tick timestamps:

```ts title="app/server/monitor/History.ts"
export const historyPoints: {
  (intervalMs: number): (historySeconds: number) => number;
  (historySeconds: number, intervalMs: number): number;
} = dual(2, (historySeconds: number, intervalMs: number): number => Math.ceil((historySeconds * 1000) / intervalMs));
```

With the defaults (`historySeconds = 300`, `pollIntervalMs = 1000`) a series holds 300 points. The settings schema
bounds `historySeconds` to 30 to 1,800 and `pollIntervalMs` to at least 250, so a series holds at most 7,200 points.
The history is only in memory: it is lost when llama-webui stops, and in development when a save of a file the
server imports restarts it.

The keys are built by `SERIES` (`packages/webui-shared/src/monitor.ts`):

| Keys                                                                         | Measure                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| `llama.genTps`, `llama.ppTps`, `llama.specAccept`                            | Generation and prompt speed, draft acceptance |
| `llama.kvPct`, `llama.reqProcessing`, `llama.reqDeferred`, `llama.busySlots` | Context use and request queue                 |
| `proc.cpuPct`, `proc.dram`                                                   | The `llama-server` process                    |
| `gpu.<key>.util`, `.vramPct`, `.pcieRx`, `.pcieTx`, `.power`, `.temp`        | Per GPU, `<key>` being `GpuSample.key`        |
| `sys.cpu`, `sys.memPct`, `sys.tempC`, `mem.read`, `mem.write`                | Machine CPU, memory, DRAM bandwidth           |
| `disk.read`, `disk.write`, `disk.modelRead`                                  | Disks, and the disk holding the model         |

Two rules keep the series aligned on the timestamps:

- Some series start at their first known value: they are pushed only when the value is a finite number or the
  series already exists. A machine without a CPU sensor never gets a `sys.tempC` series. The others exist from the
  first tick.
- Once it exists, a series moves one point per tick. An unknown value is stored as `NaN`, and `endTick()` pushes `NaN`
  to any series not fed on the tick. `NaN` becomes `null` in `points` and in the dump.

`dump()` returns a `HistoryDump`: `intervalMs`, `capacity`, `at`, `times` and one array per series, padded on the
left with `null` for a series that started after the oldest timestamp.

## How snapshots reach the browser

The monitor publishes each snapshot on the hub (`WsHub`, `app/server/ws/WsHub.ts`) as a `snapshot` WebSocket
message ([WebSocket](/developers/api/websocket/)). The hub keeps the latest one and sends it to a client right after
its `hello`, so a page has values as soon as it connects. The web app (`app/src/lib/monitor.ts`):

- reads `GET /api/monitor/history` when the WebSocket opens, and replays the snapshots that arrived during that
  request;
- appends each snapshot's `points` to its copy of the history, and trims it to `capacity`;
- reads the history again when `snapshot.history` (interval or capacity) no longer matches its copy, after a settings
  change;
- empties its copy when the `hello` message carries a different `bootId`, since the server restarted.

`GET /api/monitor/snapshot` returns the latest snapshot, or `null` before the first tick.

## Collectors

`Collectors` (`app/server/collectors/Collectors.ts`) builds every reader once, from the stored settings:

- **NVIDIA**: NVML when the `collectors.nvml` setting is on and its sampler comes up, otherwise `nvidia-smi`. A
  sampler that dies later is started again, and after three failed restarts `nvidia-smi` takes over for good (see
  [NVIDIA through NVML](#nvidia-through-nvml)); `nvidiaSource` reports the source of the moment (`nvml` or
  `nvidia-smi`).
- **AMD**: the cards found by `discoverAmdCards()` at startup.
- **Process**: one process sampler.
- **CPU and memory**, **DRAM bandwidth**, **disks**: started by `configure()` according to `collectors.system` and
  `collectors.disk`, each in a scope of its own, closed (its programs stopped) when its switch is turned off.

Every program a reader starts (the NVML sampler, `nvidia-smi`, `perf`) goes through `followProgram`
(`app/server/process/followed.ts`) on `effect/process`, in the layer's scope: the end of the layer stops them all.
A failure degrades to `null` (or an empty list), never to a failed tick: a missing file, tool or permission removes a
figure.

### `llama-server`: `/health`, `/slots`, `/metrics`

`readLlama(target)` (`app/server/llama/probes.ts`) requests `/health` through `LlamaHttp`, then, when it answered
`200`, `/slots` and `/metrics` in parallel, each with a timeout of 800 ms. `parseSlots()` (`llamacpp-js/metrics`) maps the `/slots` JSON to `SlotView`. `parsePrometheus()` reads the `/metrics` text format into
a `Map` keyed by the metric name with its labels verbatim (for example
`llamacpp:spec_decode_num_accepted_tokens_per_pos_total{position="0"}`), skipping comments and non-numeric values. The
managed server always has these endpoints, since `buildCommand()` adds `--metrics` and `--slots` (see
[Supervisor](/developers/server/supervisor/#building-the-command)).

### NVIDIA through NVML

The NVML sampler (`app/server/collectors/nvml/worker.ts`) is an Effect program of its own, because reading the PCIe
throughput counters blocks about 50 ms per GPU, and a call stuck in the driver must not hold the server: the server
kills the program (`SIGKILL`) whatever it is doing. It opens `libnvidia-ml.so.1` (or `$LLAMA_WEBUI_NVML_LIB` when
set, to test the library missing) with `dlopen()` from `node:ffi` (`nvml/symbols.ts`), calls `nvmlInit_v2`, then
writes one `sample` per interval (100 ms at least). The server speaks to it in NDJSON, one JSON value per line, on
its stdin and stdout, and both sides decode the lines with the schemas of `nvml/protocol.ts`:

| Direction                         | Message                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| server to sampler (`ToSampler`)   | `{ type: 'start', intervalMs, needs, lib? }`, then `{ type: 'configure', intervalMs, needs }` |
| sampler to server (`FromSampler`) | `{ type: 'ready' }`, `{ type: 'error', message }`, `{ type: 'sample', snapshot }`             |

`nvmlReader()` (`nvml/read.ts`) makes one sample per call. On every sample it reads, per card: utilization, memory
utilization, VRAM, temperature, power, power limit, persistence mode, graphics and memory clocks, fan, current PCIe
link, PCIe throughput (received and sent, the two calls that block) and the compute processes. What does not change is
read on the first sample that needs it, then kept: per card its UUID, name, bus ID, maximum graphics clock, maximum
PCIe link and default power limit, and the driver and CUDA driver versions. Such a value is kept once NVML gives it, or
says the card does not support it; any other failure is asked again on the next sample, and a card whose UUID fails is
left out of that sample. A hidden card is only identified, so after its first sample it costs no call. Device handles
are pointers, kept as `bigint`, read once when NVML starts; out-parameters are buffers, allocated once per reader and
reused by every sample. The typed `dlopen()` checks the `NvmlSymbols` interface against the symbol definitions.

`node:ffi` is experimental in Node 26: the sampler starts with `--disable-warning=ExperimentalWarning`, so its warning
does not print on every start. `workerPath()` (`nvml/Nvml.ts`) finds its module next to the one that starts it:

| Run                                | Sampler                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pnpm dev`, unit tests             | `nvml/worker.ts`, run as is by Node (type stripping: its imports keep their `.ts` extension) |
| `node app/.output/llama-webui.mjs` | `app/.output/server/nvml-worker.mjs`, built by Rolldown in `app/build/server.ts`             |

The sampler ends at the end of its stdin, so a sampler whose server is gone, even killed, exits. It costs a process
of its own, about 116 MiB of RSS with NVML loaded.

On the server's side, `startNvml()` (`nvml/Nvml.ts`) answers none when the sampler reports an error, exits, or is
not ready within 3 seconds, and `Collectors` falls back to `nvidia-smi`. After `ready`, an `error`, an exit or a line
that is not a message clears its readings and kills it, and its `died` returns. `Collectors` then starts a new sampler,
with the last interval and hidden cards, after a pause of 1 s, then 5 s, then 30 s (`nvmlRestartDelays`), and logs a
warning at each one. A sampler that sampled for 5 minutes (`nvmlHealthyAfter`) gives the three restarts back. When
they are spent, `nvidia-smi` is started and stays the source until llama-webui restarts. The `nvidia` reader of
`Collectors` is a facade on the source of the moment, so the monitor sees a switch at its next tick. A sample older than three intervals is ignored (`isFresh()`): a sampler stuck in an NVML call
must not pass old numbers off as live ones. When NVML cannot report a process's memory (`NVML_VALUE_NOT_AVAILABLE`,
for example with MIG), the card's VRAM in use is counted instead, as an upper bound.

### NVIDIA through nvidia-smi

The `nvidia-smi` reader keeps one long-running `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=… --format=csv,noheader,nounits -lms <interval>`
stream for utilization, VRAM, temperature, power and bus ID: no PCIe throughput, clocks or fans. The process list
comes from a separate `nvidia-smi --query-compute-apps=pid,gpu_uuid,used_memory` call, one at a time, killed after
3 seconds (the timeout kills it). The monitor awaits it on the first tick of a new PID, then runs it in the
background every 5 ticks (`appsEvery` of `Monitor.layerWith`). The binary is `nvidia-smi`, or `$LLAMA_WEBUI_NVIDIA_SMI` when set (to test the tool missing).

### AMD through amdgpu

`discoverAmdCards()` lists `/sys/class/drm/card<N>` entries whose `device/vendor` is `0x1002`, with their hwmon
folder, their DRM nodes (`card<N>`, `renderD<N>`), the PCIe link that leads to the slot, and a name from `pci.ids`.
`readAmdCard()` reads `gpu_busy_percent`, `mem_info_vram_used` and `mem_info_vram_total`, `mem_busy_percent`, the
link speed and width, `pp_dpm_sclk` and `pp_dpm_mclk`, and from hwmon the temperature, power, power cap and fan.

- A card whose `power/runtime_status` is `suspended` is not read: reading its attributes would wake it up.
- The process VRAM comes from `/proc/<pid>/fdinfo`: entries with `drm-driver: amdgpu` give `drm-pdev` and
  `drm-memory-vram`, counted once per `drm-pdev` and `drm-client-id`. Descriptors open on a hidden or suspended card's
  DRM node are recognized by their `/proc/<pid>/fd` link and their fdinfo is not read.

### `/proc`: process, CPU and memory

- The process sampler reads `utime` and `stime` from `/proc/<pid>/stat` (100 ticks per second) and `VmRSS` from
  `/proc/<pid>/status`. `cpuPct` is the CPU time used since the previous sample over the elapsed time, so it exceeds
  100 when several cores work.
- The system sampler reads `/proc/stat` (total and per-core usage), `/proc/meminfo`, `/proc/loadavg`, the
  average `scaling_cur_freq` of the cores, and the CPU temperature from the first hwmon named `k10temp` or `coretemp`.

### Disks

`physicalDisks()` lists `/sys/block` entries backed by a device, without `loop`, `ram` and `zram`, once when the disk
collector is turned on. The disk sampler reads `/proc/diskstats` (sectors of 512 bytes) and returns the read and write
rates per disk and in total.

The disk that holds the model is found by `resolveModelDisk(path)`: it maps the file's device number to
`/sys/dev/block/<major>:<minor>`, and from a partition to its parent disk. It returns `null` when the disk is unknown
(tmpfs, btrfs, NFS, missing file). The monitor runs that lookup in a fiber of its own, never awaited, once per
profile, and retries a failed one after 30 seconds (`modelDiskRetry` of `Monitor.layerWith`): a `stat` on a dead
network mount must never block a tick. The model path is the profile's, from `profilePaths()`.

### DRAM bandwidth (perf)

The DRAM bandwidth sampler (`membw.ts`) runs two `perf stat -I <interval> -x , --log-fd 1` processes:

- machine-wide, `-a -e umc_cas_cmd.rd,umc_cas_cmd.wr`: the AMD memory controller's CAS commands, which count every
  access to DRAM (CPU, GPU DMA over PCIe, NVMe);
- for `llama-server`, `-p <pid> -e ls_any_fills_from_sys.dram_io_all`: cache lines the process fetched from DRAM.

Each event counts 64 bytes (`BYTES_PER_EVENT`). It works without root when `perf_event_paranoid` is at most 0, or with
`CAP_PERFMON` on `perf`. When `perf` is missing, refuses the event, or exits, the figure becomes unknown and the
stream is retried no sooner than 60 seconds later. A PID `perf` could never attach to is not retried. A
rate older than three intervals counts as unknown. The sampler follows the CPU and memory switch.
[Monitor DRAM bandwidth](/docs/guides/dram-bandwidth/) explains the setup for users.

## What the GPU readers read

`GpuNeeds` (`app/server/collectors/gpu.ts`) tells the GPU readers what to read:

```ts title="app/server/collectors/gpu.ts"
export interface GpuNeeds {
  /** Hidden cards (PCI bus ids): not read. */
  readonly skipGpus: ReadonlyArray<string>;
}
```

`gpuNeeds(hiddenGpus)` measures everything, the PCIe throughput included, and only skips the hidden cards.
`gpuSkipped(needs, busId)` is the single test the NVML sampler and the monitor apply: a skipped card is only identified
(key, vendor, name, bus ID).

## Model placement

`Placement` (`app/server/monitor/Placement.ts`) reads how the loaded model is split across devices from `llama-server`'s startup log lines, which
the API does not expose: `using device … (<bus id>)`, `<device> model|KV|RS|compute buffer size = … MiB`,
`offloaded N/M layers to GPU`. `parsePlacement()` (`placementLog.ts`) starts from the last launch line (`LAUNCH_LOG_PREFIX`) in the log
ring, skips the `-fit` probes, counts compute buffers per context, and stops at the second "offloaded" line, which
belongs to a draft model. `llama-server` logs the buffer sizes from `-lv 4` on, which `buildCommand()` adds to a
profile without a verbosity of its own.

With `-sm tensor`, the buffers are logged on one `Meta()` device, at the size of its largest per-device part
(`ggml-backend-meta.cpp` reports the maximum). `spreadMeta()` replaces them with one buffer per member, read from the
`llama_prepare_model_devices: - device N: <name>` lines: model and cache scaled by `ts[i] / max(ts)` (`-ts` from the
launch line, equal parts without it), the whole compute buffer on each, and `estimated: true`.

Two things come from lines that are not `llama-server`'s own, read over the whole launch (NCCL starts with the
context, after the lines of a draft model):

- **Peer-to-peer** (`parseP2p()`). llama.cpp enables CUDA peer-to-peer as soon as `GGML_CUDA_P2P` exists and logs
  nothing, so the supervisor writes `P2P_LOG_LINE` after the launch line when the variable is in the server's
  environment. NCCL, built in and run with `NCCL_DEBUG=INFO`, says what really happens, which depends on the same
  driver capability: a `Channel … via P2P/…` line gives `active`; with the variable set, `P2P is disabled between
connected GPUs` or a channel `via SHM/…` gives `unavailable`, and no NCCL line gives `requested`.
- **PCI addresses of CUDA devices** (`ncclBusIds()`). NCCL's `ncclCommInitAll … cudaDev N nvmlDev N busId X` lines
  give the address of `CUDA<N>` as hex digits (`3000` is `0000:03:00.0`). An address from a `using device` line wins;
  with `-sm tensor`, which only logs the `Meta()` device, this is the only one.

`Placement` reads the log ring when its layer is built, and keeps the last placement it read with the PID of its
server, so once the launch lines have left the ring it still knows the placement of the same PID. It follows the new
log lines (only those that can change the result, `RELEVANT`) and the supervisor's state, and publishes each change
as a `placement` message.
An external server has no placement: it writes nothing to the ring.

## Add a collector

1. Write the reader in `app/server/collectors/<name>.ts`: pure parse functions for the raw text, and a sampler made
   by an effect (`make<Name>Sampler()`) that reads through `FileSystem`, with its roots (`procRoot`, `sysRoot`) as
   options so tests run on fixtures. A program goes through `followProgram`. It gives `null` on any failure.
2. Add its schema to `packages/webui-shared/src/monitor.ts` as an optional or nullable field of `Snapshot` (or of
   `SystemSample`), and its series keys to `SERIES`.
3. Build it in `Collectors`: at its construction if it is always on, in `configure()` if a switch in `collectors`
   (`packages/webui-shared/src/settings.ts`) controls it.
4. Read it in `Monitor.tick`, and push its series and put its value in the snapshot in `endTick()` (`tick.ts`).
5. Save: `pnpm dev` restarts the server, and the new collector runs from the first tick.

The pages that show the new figure ([System](/docs/system/), [Dashboard](/docs/dashboard/)) change in the same pull
request, with this page. See also [How-to guides](/developers/contributing/how-to/).
