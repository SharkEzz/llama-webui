The Topology page draws your machine: the CPU, the memory, the GPUs and NVMe drives on their PCIe links, with the
measured traffic flowing along each link. GPUs and memory show the memory they hold, split by what the loaded model
takes in them. Use it to see at a glance where a model lives, which component works and which link is busy.

## Two views

The control at the top of the page, **Cards** or **Board**, switches between two drawings of the same machine, with
the same blocks, the same figures and the same links. The browser remembers the one you chose.

- **Cards** is a diagram: the **CPU** on top with the **Memory** at its left and an integrated GPU at its right, a row
  below with the devices on the CPU's own PCIe lanes (GPUs, then NVMe drives, then chipsets), and one row further the
  devices connected through each **chipset**. Curves link a device to what it hangs off.
- **Board** is the plan of a motherboard seen from above: the socket with the memory modules at its right, the CPU's
  drives and an integrated GPU at its left, one slot per GPU below (those of the CPU first, then those of a chipset),
  the chipsets at the lower right with their drives. Tracks run at right angles and never cross.

<Callout type="note">

The board is a convention, not a picture of your motherboard: llama-webui knows what each device hangs off, not where
its slot is. The number of memory modules is the real one when the kernel reads it (see **DIMMs** below); otherwise
the board fills two of its four slots.

</Callout>

The hardware is read from the kernel each time you open the page, so the link states are current (links slow down
when idle to save power). Every display controller is drawn, integrated GPUs included, and every NVMe drive. SATA
drives and other PCIe devices are not.

Each GPU gets its own color, used again in the legend and the model split. The CPU uses the accent color and the
memory its own; drives and chipsets are neutral.

The drawing starts whole in the room it has, at the left of the side column: it grows on a large screen (up to twice its size) and
shrinks with the window. From there you look closer with the mouse:

- The **wheel** zooms in and out around the pointer, up to four times the drawing's size and back to the whole
  drawing.
- A **drag** moves the drawing in its frame. A press that does not move stays a click, on a block for example.
- Three buttons at the lower right of the frame do the same without a wheel: **Zoom in**, **Reset zoom**, which shows
  the whole drawing again, and **Zoom out**.

Switching between **Cards** and **Board** shows the whole drawing again.

## The blocks

### GPUs and memory

A GPU and the memory have a wide bar: the memory in use, on a linear scale, so a bar half full is a card with half
its VRAM taken. The figure is the measured one, the same as on the [System](/docs/system/) page. On the board, each
memory module is an upright bar, and all of them show the level of the whole memory.

In a GPU, the bar is split in layers, in this order:

| Layer               | What it is                                                                        | Color                 |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| **Model**           | The weights of the loaded model on this card                                      | The card's color      |
| **KV cache**        | The context kept on this card                                                     | A lighter shade       |
| **Compute buffers** | Working memory of `llama-server` on this card                                     | A lighter shade still |
| **Other VRAM**      | Everything else in use: the desktop, other programs, a draft model or a projector | Gray                  |

The three first sizes come from `llama-server`'s logs (see [How the model split is found](#how-the-model-split-is-found)).
When they add up to more than the VRAM in use, while the model is still loading or with the estimate of `-sm tensor`,
they shrink together: the bar never exceeds the measurement. Without a known split, the bar has a single gray layer.
Hover any segment of a GPU bar, or its free remainder, to see its label, size and share of VRAM. A layer shrunk to
the measurement shows both sizes, as in `3.9 GiB of 11.2 GiB`: the part of the bar it takes, then the size announced,
which is the one in the block's details.

The memory has a single layer, the RAM in use. Weights mapped from the disk (`mmap`) sit in the page cache, which is
not counted as used, so the model has no layer of its own there: its size is in the block's details, as **Model in
RAM**.

From 85% full, the bar is outlined and its percentage turns amber; from 95%, red. Hidden and sleeping GPUs are dimmed
and say **Hidden in Settings** or **Suspended, not read** instead of their figures (see
[Sleeping and hidden GPUs](/docs/system/#sleeping-and-hidden-gpus)).

### What a block shows

| Block      | On the block                                                                                                                |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| GPU        | Utilization (with a thin bar in the Cards view), temperature, power, the VRAM in use, its total and share                   |
| CPU        | CPU load of the machine, one tile per thread lit with the load of that thread, temperature, and frequency in the Cards view |
| Memory     | Share of the RAM in use, the RAM in use and its total                                                                       |
| NVMe drive | **Read** and **Write** in the Cards view; on the board, a light that brightens with its activity                            |
| Chipset    | Number of devices behind it, and its current link to the CPU in the Cards view                                              |

The tiles of the CPU show how many threads work, not only the average.

### Details

Select a block, with a click or with <Kbd>Enter</Kbd> once it has the focus, to open all its figures beside it.
The block stays outlined while its details are open, and they keep following the measurements. Select another
block to move them there. Select the block again, press <Kbd>Esc</Kbd>, click elsewhere or use **Close the
details** to close them.

| Block      | Details                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| GPU        | **Utilization**, **VRAM**, the **Model**, **KV cache** and **Compute buffers** sizes it holds, **Other VRAM**, **llama-server (VRAM)**, **Temperature**, **Power**, **Memory controller**, **Fan**, **Power limit**, **Graphics clock**, **Memory clock**, **PCIe link**, **Host → GPU** and **GPU → host** traffic, **Peer-to-peer** |
| CPU        | **Load**, **llama-server** CPU use (one full core is 100%: it can exceed 100%), **Frequency**, **Temperature**, **Load average** (1, 5 and 15 minutes), **Cores**, **NUMA**                                                                                                                                                           |
| Memory     | **Used**, **Cached**, DRAM **Read** and **Write** (or **Bandwidth** not measured), **llama-server (DRAM)**, **llama-server (RSS)**, **Model in RAM**, **Swap**, **DIMMs** with their temperature                                                                                                                                      |
| NVMe drive | **Read**, **Write**, **Capacity**, **PCIe link**, and **Role** when it holds the running model                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Chipset    | **CPU link** (current and maximum), number of **Devices** behind it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

A detail appears only when it is known: what a GPU reports depends on its driver (see
[What each source reports](/docs/system/#what-each-source-reports)). **Power limit** adds the factory value when the limit was lowered.
**DIMMs** needs memory modules with a temperature sensor the kernel reads (the `spd5118` driver, for DDR5 modules).

## Traffic on the links

Links join the blocks: curves between the cards, tracks on the board.

- A link's thickness shows the PCIe capacity of the link at its maximum generation and width, so a Gen5 x16 slot looks
  thicker than a Gen4 x4 one.
- The **CPU ↔ memory** link has a fixed thickness.
- A thin, faint link is an internal connection without a PCIe link to report: the one of a GPU inside the processor,
  which is in no slot. It carries nothing and has no label.

Each PCIe link has a label with its current state, such as `Gen4 x8`: a card that trained below what its slot can do
shows there. In the Cards view the label sits on the link, above the card it reaches; on the board, in the margin
beside the slot or the drive.

In the **Board** view, each card in a slot has the contacts of its connector drawn under it, as long as an x16 one.
The contacts its link uses are gold and the others gray: all of them at x16, half at x8, a quarter at x4.

### Streaks

Streaks travel along a link when traffic is measured on it, toward the device on one lane and back on the other.
More traffic means more streaks, and faster ones, on a logarithmic scale, so 50 MiB/s stays visible next to 20 GiB/s.
The link itself takes its color, stronger with more traffic. Streaks show that something flows and roughly how much;
the exact figure is on the link's label. At rest, the same 8 MiB/s floor as for the figures applies (see below): the
background traffic of an idle GPU leaves its link still.

### Throughput

A link that carries traffic adds the exact throughput in each direction to its label:

| Link             | Lines of the label        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------- |
| CPU ↔ GPU        | **→ GPU**, **→ host**     |
| CPU ↔ NVMe drive | **read**, **write**       |
| CPU ↔ memory     | **read**, **write**       |
| CPU ↔ chipset    | **→ devices**, **→ host** |

While `llama-server` loads a model, processes a prompt or generates, every link with measured traffic shows its
figures. At rest, a link shows its traffic, streaks and figures, only from 8 MiB/s: an idle GPU exchanges a few MiB/s
with the host, which would otherwise leave streaks running all the time. The details of a block always give them.

### A link near its limit

Each direction of a PCIe link has the full capacity of the link in its current state (a link that slowed down to Gen1
saturates much sooner than at its maximum). When the busier direction reaches 25% of that capacity, the label adds
the percentage, such as **87% of capacity**. The link, its streaks and that line turn amber from 70% and red from 90%.

This mostly shows while a model loads: reading it from an NVMe drive and sending it to the GPUs can fill a link.
During a generation, a GPU link usually stays under 1% of its capacity. A chipset link is measured against its own
link to the CPU, with the sum of the devices behind it: two busy devices on one chipset can saturate it together.

### What is measured

| Link             | Measured from                                                                             | Without a measurement                                |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| CPU ↔ NVIDIA GPU | The card's PCIe counters, through NVML, read every 5 seconds                              | No streaks, no figures (NVIDIA through `nvidia-smi`) |
| CPU ↔ AMD GPU    | Not measured: the `amdgpu` driver does not report PCIe traffic                            | No streaks, no figures                               |
| CPU ↔ NVMe drive | The drive's read and write counters (**Disks** collector)                                 | No streaks, no figures                               |
| CPU ↔ chipset    | The sum of the devices behind it                                                          | No streaks, no figures                               |
| CPU ↔ memory     | DRAM traffic through `perf` (AMD Zen 4 and Zen 5), writes on one lane, reads on the other | A link whose color follows CPU activity, no streaks  |

Without a DRAM measurement, the memory link is colored by `llama-server`'s CPU use when a server runs, and by the
machine's CPU load otherwise: it has no streaks, since nothing says which way the bytes go.
[Monitor DRAM bandwidth](/docs/guides/dram-bandwidth/) explains how to get the real figure.

### Peer-to-peer between GPUs

With several GPUs on one backend, `llama-server` can let them exchange directly instead of through system memory
(CUDA peer-to-peer). It is requested by setting the `GGML_CUDA_P2P` variable in the profile's environment, and it
only works when the driver allows it, which is usually limited to workstation and datacenter cards.

The page shows what is known of it for the cards that hold the model:

| State                     | How it is known                                                                                       | On the page                                     |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Active**                | NCCL reports its channels going from GPU to GPU (`via P2P/…`)                                         | A dotted link between the cards, tagged **P2P** |
| **Requested**             | `GGML_CUDA_P2P` is set and nothing says whether the driver allows it                                  | A fainter dotted link, tagged **P2P requested** |
| **Refused by the driver** | `GGML_CUDA_P2P` is set and NCCL reports peer-to-peer disabled, its channels going through host memory | No link: the cards do not exchange directly     |

The state is also written under the model split and in each card's details. Only NCCL tells the first and the last
apart, and it only writes these lines with `NCCL_DEBUG=INFO` in the profile's environment, on a `llama-server` built
with NCCL: add that variable to a profile to know whether your cards really use peer-to-peer.

The link is dotted and carries no streaks: nothing measures that traffic apart. It is part of the figures of each
card's own PCIe link.

## The side column

A column at the right of the drawing holds the title and the view control, then the **Phase** and **Model** cards and
the **Legend** button. It stays there in both views. Only a window too narrow for it, a phone or a small tablet, shows
these cards after the drawing: scroll the page to read them.

### Phase

The **Phase** card shows what `llama-server` is doing, with the current **Generation** and **Prompt** speeds in
tok/s (the same as on the [Dashboard](/docs/dashboard/)).

| Phase          | Meaning                                                                              |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Stopped**    | No server runs, or it is stopping or has crashed.                                    |
| **Loading**    | The server is starting and loading its model (with stage and percentage when known). |
| **Sleeping**   | The server is in idle sleep (`--sleep-idle-seconds`): VRAM is released until wake.   |
| **Idle**       | The server is ready and no slot is busy.                                             |
| **Prompt**     | A busy slot has not generated its first token yet: the prompt is processed.          |
| **Generating** | Tokens are being generated.                                                          |

### Model

The **Model** card shows how the model's weights are split. A bar and a list give each device's share, with its size
and percentage.

- GPUs appear under `llama-server`'s device names: `CUDA0`, `ROCm0`, `Vulkan1`…
- **Host (RAM)** sums everything kept in system memory, including weights mapped from the disk (`mmap`).

The line below gives the total size and the **offloaded layers** count, such as `66/66`. Only the main model is
counted: a draft model and a multimodal projector are left out. The KV cache is not in the bar; each GPU's share
shows as a layer of its bar, with its size in its details.

When the split is not known, the card says why:

- **No managed server: split unknown.** No server runs.
- **Loading: the split arrives with the logs.** The server is still reading the model.
- **Model is sleeping: VRAM released until the next request.** The server is in idle sleep.
- **Split unknown: an external server without /models/sse, or a profile whose -lv is below 4.** Neither buffer logs nor
  `/models/sse` memory breakdown are available.

With `-sm tensor`, the card adds **estimate (tensor parallel)**: see below. When peer-to-peer is requested or
reported, a last line gives its state.

### Legend

The **Legend** button opens the legend: what the bars, their layers, the streaks, the labels of the links and the
alert colors mean, whether the memory link shows measured DRAM traffic or CPU activity, and the color of each GPU.

## How the model split is found

`llama-server` does not report where it put the model through its API, but it writes it to its output while loading.
llama-webui reads those lines from the [logs](/docs/logs/) of the last launch:

- which device each backend uses, with its PCI address, which ties `CUDA0` to a block of the drawing;
- the size of the model, KV cache and compute buffers on each device;
- the number of layers offloaded to the GPUs.

`llama-server` only writes the buffer sizes from log verbosity 4 on, so llama-webui starts it with `-lv 4` unless the
profile sets a verbosity itself (`-lv`, `-v` or `LLAMA_ARG_LOG_VERBOSITY`). The card and the bars fill in while the
model loads and update live. When startup buffer logs are missing (an external server or a profile with `-lv < 4`),
llama-webui falls back to the memory breakdown sent on `/models/sse` (single-model mode, llama.cpp PR #32398), marking
the card **from /models/sse** when layer counts are unavailable. If neither logs nor an unambiguous single-GPU match
give a device's PCI address, its slice is shown in gray and its bar keeps a single gray layer.

With `-sm tensor` (tensor parallelism), `llama-server` groups the GPUs into one `Meta()` device and logs each buffer at
the size of its largest part. llama-webui spreads it over the GPUs listed in the logs by `-ts` (equal parts without
it), and the card says **estimate (tensor parallel)**. `llama-server` gives no PCI address for these GPUs: their
slices are gray and their bars keep a single layer, unless the profile sets `NCCL_DEBUG=INFO`, with which NCCL logs
the address of each CUDA device.

## Motion and reduced motion

The streaks are the only thing that moves between two measurements: they are drawn 20 times a second, and only while
a link carries traffic worth showing. Everything else changes once a second, when a new measurement arrives.

With the system's reduced motion setting on, there are no streaks: a busy link is dashed and more strongly colored
instead, and the legend says **Reduced motion: no streaks, a busy link is dashed.** The figures and the bars stay.

## Keyboard

Every block is a button: <Kbd>Tab</Kbd> goes from one to the next, <Kbd>Enter</Kbd> or <Kbd>Space</Kbd> opens its
details and moves the focus into them, and <Kbd>Esc</Kbd> closes them and gives the focus back to the block. In a
zoomed drawing, a block that takes the focus comes into view. The three zoom buttons follow the blocks in the tab
order.

## If the hardware cannot be read

If the page cannot load the hardware graph from llama-webui, the phase card shows **Topology unreadable (API
unavailable).**

## Without a running model

The page works without a server: it shows the hardware with its live traffic and load, each GPU's bar filled to the
VRAM in use in a single gray layer, the phase is **Stopped**, and the model card says **No managed server: split
unknown.** Start a profile from the [Server](/docs/server/) page to see the bars fill layer by layer as the model
loads.
